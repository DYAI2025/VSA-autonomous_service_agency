## Slide 1: Company Purpose (The "One-Liner")

### Objective
Communicate your company's essence in a single, powerful sentence.

### Content Structure
- **Company Name** + **What you do** + **For whom** + **Why it matters**

### Template
```
[Company Name] helps [target customer] [solve key problem] by [unique approach].
```

### Examples
- "Stripe helps internet businesses accept payments by building economic infrastructure."
- "Airbnb helps people belong anywhere by creating a world where anyone can belong anywhere."

### Key Principles
- Maximum 20 words
- No jargon or buzzwords
- Clear value proposition
- Memorable and repeatable

### Design Tips
- Large, bold company logo
- Clean, minimal design
- One sentence prominently displayed
- Optional: Tagline or sub-headline

## Slide 2: The Problem (The "Villain")

### Objective
Make investors feel the pain your customers experience.

### Content Structure
- **Current Situation**: What's broken or inefficient
- **Who Suffers**: Specific customer segment
- **The Pain**: Quantitative and qualitative impact
- **The Gap**: Why existing solutions fail

### Template
```
Today, [target customers] struggle with [specific problem].
This costs them [quantified impact: time, money, opportunities].
Existing solutions [fail because of specific reason].
```

### Key Elements
- Use real customer quotes if possible
- Include data points (market size, cost of problem)
- Make it visceral and emotional
- Show you understand customer pain deeply

### Design Tips
- Problem statement in large text
- Supporting statistics or quotes
- Simple iconography or imagery
- Dark background with white text for impact

## Slide 3: The Solution (The "Hero")

### Objective
Present your solution as the obvious answer to the problem.

### Content Structure
- **Your Solution**: What you've built
- **How It Works**: Simple explanation (3 steps max)
- **Why It's Better**: Key differentiators
- **The "Aha" Moment**: Clear value proposition

### Template
```
We've built [product name] that [solves the problem] by [unique mechanism].
Unlike [alternatives], we [key differentiator].
The result: [tangible benefit for customer].
```

### Key Elements
- Product screenshot or simple diagram
- 3-step "how it works" flow
- Clear before/after comparison
- One key differentiator highlighted

### Design Tips
- Product visual as hero element
- Simple process flow diagram
- Before/after comparison
- Bright, optimistic colors

---

## Slide 4: Why Now (The "Timing Thesis")

### Objective
Explain why this is the right moment for your solution.

### Content Structure
- **Market Shift**: What's changing in the world
- **Technology Enabler**: Why this is possible now
- **Cultural Change**: Why customer behavior is shifting
- **Window of Opportunity**: Why timing is critical

### Template
```
[Market shift] is creating [opportunity].
New technology (specific) makes this possible for the first time.
Customer behavior is changing because [specific trend].
This creates a [timeframe] window to capture this market.
```

### Key Elements
- Specific market trends with data
- Technology breakthrough enabling solution
- Cultural or behavioral shifts
- Urgency/timeline for market entry

### Design Tips
- Timeline showing evolution
- Trend arrows or growth charts
- Technology icons or illustrations
- Callout for "why now"

## Slide 5: Market Size (The "Opportunity")

### Objective
Demonstrate a large, growing market opportunity.

### Content Structure
- **TAM** (Total Addressable Market): Total possible market
- **SAM** (Serviceable Addressable Market): Reachable market
- **SOM** (Serviceable Obtainable Market): Realistic near-term market
- **Growth Rate**: Market growth trajectory

### Template
```
TAM: €[X]B (total [industry] market)
SAM: €[Y]B ([specific segment] we can reach)
SOM: €[Z]M (realistic 3-5 year target)
Market growing at [X]% annually driven by [factors]
```

### Key Elements
- Bottom-up calculation (not top-down)
- Clear assumptions explained
- Growth drivers identified
- Realistic near-term targets

### Design Tips
- Three concentric circles or stacked bars
- Clear labels for TAM/SAM/SOM
- Growth arrow or trend line
- Supporting data points

## Slide 6: Competition (The "Landscape")

### Objective
Show you understand the competitive landscape and your positioning.

### Content Structure
- **Competitive Landscape**: Major players and segments
- **Your Position**: Where you fit in the landscape
- **Key Differentiators**: 2-3 things that make you unique
- **Defensibility**: Why you can win and sustain advantage

### Template
```
Market landscape includes [key competitors].
We position ourselves as [your category].
Key differentiators:
1. [Differentiator 1]
2. [Differentiator 2]
3. [Differentiator 3]
Defensible because [moats: IP, network effects, data, etc.]
```

### Key Elements
- 2x2 matrix or competitive landscape map
- Honest acknowledgment of competitors
- Clear, defensible differentiation
- Specific competitive advantages

### Design Tips
- 2x2 matrix with your company highlighted
- Competitor logos or names
- Your differentiators called out
- Clean, professional layout

## Slide 7: Product (The "Proof")

### Objective
Show your product in action with tangible evidence.

### Content Structure
- **Product Overview**: What it is and key features
- **Screenshots/Demo**: Visual proof of product
- **Key Metrics**: Usage, engagement, performance data
- **Customer Validation**: Testimonials or case studies

### Template
```
Our product [name] delivers [key benefits].
Key features: [3-5 most important features]
[Product screenshots or demo link]
Traction: [key metrics - users, revenue, engagement]
Customer feedback: "[quote from satisfied customer]"
```

### Key Elements
- High-quality product screenshots
- Key features (not exhaustive list)
- Metrics showing product-market fit
- Social proof and validation

### Design Tips
- Large product screenshots
- Feature callouts with labels
- Metrics with simple charts
- Customer quote in callout box

---

## Slide 8: Business Model (The "Economics")

### Objective
Explain how you make money and unit economics.

### Content Structure
- **Revenue Model**: How you generate revenue
- **Pricing**: Price points and packages
- **Unit Economics**: CAC, LTV, payback period
- **Revenue Drivers**: Key growth levers

### Template
```
Revenue model: [subscription/transaction/marketplace]
Pricing: starting at €[X]/month
Unit economics:
- CAC: €[X]
- LTV: €[Y] (LTV:CAC ratio of [X]:1)
- Payback period: [X] months
Growth drivers: [3 key factors]
```

### Key Elements
- Clear revenue model explanation
- Pricing strategy and rationale
- Healthy unit economics (LTV:CAC > 3:1)
- Understanding of growth levers

### Design Tips
- Simple business model diagram
- Pricing tiers or packages
- Unit economics in simple table
- Growth funnel visualization

## Slide 9: Team (The "People")

### Objective
Convince investors you're the right team to execute this vision.

### Content Structure
- **Founders**: Background and relevant experience
- **Key Hires**: Critical team members and their expertise
- **Advisors/Board**: Notable advisors or investors
- **Why This Team**: Unique qualifications for this specific opportunity

### Template
```
Founder 1: [Name] - [Role] - [relevant background/experience]
Founder 2: [Name] - [Role] - [relevant background/experience]
Key hires: [2-3 critical team members]
Advisors: [notable advisors or early investors]
Why us: [specific qualifications for this opportunity]
```

### Key Elements
- Relevant domain expertise
- Previous successes or relevant experience
- Founder-market fit
- Complementary skill sets

### Design Tips
- Professional headshots
- Clean bio format with key highlights
- Company logos of previous employers
- Simple, elegant layout

---

## Slide 10: Financials & The Ask (The "Request")

### Objective
Present your financial projections and funding request.

### Content Structure
- **Financial Projections**: 3-year revenue and key metrics
- **Use of Funds**: How you'll use the investment
- **The Ask**: Amount being raised and key terms
- **Milestones**: What you'll achieve with this funding

### Template
```
Financial projections (3 years):
Year 1: €[X]M revenue, [key metrics]
Year 2: €[Y]M revenue, [key metrics]
Year 3: €[Z]M revenue, [key metrics]

Use of funds:
- Product development: [X]%
- Sales/marketing: [Y]%
- Team expansion: [Z]%

Raising €[X]M to achieve [key milestones]
```

### Key Elements
- Realistic but ambitious projections
- Clear use of funds breakdown
- Specific funding ask
- Measurable milestones

### Design Tips
- Simple revenue growth chart
- Use of funds pie chart
- Key milestones timeline
- Clean numbers presentation

## Design Best Practices

### Overall Principles
- **Consistent branding**: Colors, fonts, logo placement
- **Simple layout**: One main idea per slide
- **Large text**: Minimum 30pt font size
- **High contrast**: Easy to read in all lighting
- **Professional quality**: No typos, alignment issues

### Visual Elements
- **Data visualization**: Simple charts, avoid clutter
- **Images**: High-quality, relevant, not generic stock photos
- **White space**: Don't crowd slides
- **Color scheme**: 2-3 primary colors max
- **Animations**: Minimal, professional only

### Length Guidelines
- **Total slides**: 10-12 slides maximum
- **Presentation time**: 18-22 minutes
- **Font size**: Minimum 30pt body text
- **Words per slide**: 20-30 words maximum

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

### Content Mistakes
- Too much text on slides
- Jargon or technical complexity
- Unrealistic financial projections
- Vague or generic value propositions
- Missing competitive landscape

### Design Mistakes
- Inconsistent branding
- Too small font size
- Poor color contrast
- Generic stock photos
- Cluttered layouts

### Strategic Mistakes
- Not addressing the "why now"
- Weak team presentation
- Unrealistic market size claims
- Missing use of funds clarity
- No clear call-to-action

## Sequoia Framework Principles

### Core Philosophy
1. **Clarity over cleverness**: Simple, direct communication
2. **Storytelling**: Emotional connection before data dump
3. **Evidence**: Data and proof to back claims
4. **Focus**: One clear narrative thread throughout
5. **Respect**: Investor's time and intelligence

### The "Why" Behind the Structure
- **Company Purpose**: Forces clarity and focus
- **Problem**: Establishes emotional connection
- **Solution**: Shows your unique approach
- **Why Now**: Creates urgency and timing thesis
- **Market**: Demonstrates opportunity size
- **Competition**: Shows market awareness
- **Product**: Proves you can execute
- **Business Model**: Shows economic viability
- **Team**: Proves you can win
- **Financials/Ask**: Clear path forward
