## Semantic Memory: Generalized Patterns

### Pattern: Pitch Deck Content Hierarchy

**Optimal Structure for Seed Stage**:
1. Title Slide (brand positioning)
2. Problem Statement (market pain)
3. Solution (unique value proposition)
4. Market Opportunity (size and growth)
5. Technology (competitive advantage)
6. Product (features and roadmap)
7. Business Model (revenue streams)
8. Traction (validation metrics)
9. Competitive Landscape (differentiation)
10. Go-to-Market (execution strategy)
11. Team (capability demonstration)
12. Financials (use of funds)
13. Milestones (clear timeline)
14. Investment Ask (specific terms)
15. Contact (call to action)

**Pattern Extension**: Later stages add depth to each section with specific metrics and projections.

### Pattern: Financial Progression Logic

**Seed Stage Financials**:
- Focus: Use of funds allocation
- Timeline: 18-24 month runway
- Metrics: User acquisition targets
- Valuation: €1-3M range

**Series A Financials**:
- Focus: Revenue trajectory and unit economics
- Timeline: 24 months to profitability
- Metrics: CAC, LTV, retention rates
- Valuation: €5-10M range

**Series B Financials**:
- Focus: Global expansion and IPO pathway
- Timeline: 30 months to public markets
- Metrics: Market share, enterprise penetration
- Valuation: €20-40M range

**Anti-Pattern**: Using early-stage financial logic for later-stage pitches reduces credibility.

### Pattern: Competitive Moat Communication

**Effective Moat Presentation**:
1. **Mathematical Foundation**: Quantifiable technical advantage
2. **Dual-System Matrix**: Unique intellectual property
3. **AI-Augmented Narrative**: Scalable personalization
4. **Data Network Effects**: User-driven improvement
5. **Regulatory Compliance**: Barrier to entry
6. **Partnership Ecosystem**: Market positioning

**Learning**: Moats should be presented as layered defenses, not single advantages.
